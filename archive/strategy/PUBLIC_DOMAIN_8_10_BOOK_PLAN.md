# Public-Domain Catalog Plan — the first ten titles

> Researched 2026-08-29 against primary sources. **[FACT]** = verified against a cited source · **[INFER]** = reasoning from verified facts · **[REC]** = recommendation.
>
> **This is a researched, rights-checked acquisition plan — not finished books.** Section 34 of the brief asks for the editions to be *produced*: sources obtained, differentiated editions created, covers made, samples cut, catalog entries built. That is roughly a book-production project per title and has **not** been done. What follows is everything that must be settled *before* production, done properly, so production can start without re-litigating rights.

---

## Three findings that should change the strategy before you pick a title

### 1. KDP does not accept public-domain illustrations as differentiation **[FACT]**

Amazon's *Publishing Public Domain Content* policy accepts exactly three differentiators:

- **(Translated)** — an original translation
- **(Annotated)** — original annotations: study guides, critique, biography, historical context
- **(Illustrated)** — *"10 or more original illustrations relevant to the book"* — explicitly **original, not sourced from other public-domain material**

Explicitly rejected: a linked table of contents, formatting improvements, bundling as a "collection", price, sales rank.

*Source: [KDP: Publishing Public Domain Content](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200743940), [KDP Content Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390) — checked 2026-08-29.*

**This kills the most obvious plan.** Reprinting Rackham's Grimm or Batten's Celtic fairy tales earns *zero* differentiation credit — every competitor has the same plates and already uses them.

### 2. Public-domain content is capped at the 35% royalty tier **[FACT]**

*"Works in the public domain or consisting primarily of public domain content are only eligible for the 35% royalty option."* An added introduction or notes is explicitly **not** enough; only *substantial* original content, or an original translation, makes a book "not primarily public domain."

*Source: [KDP eBook Royalties](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200644210) — checked 2026-08-29.*

**[INFER]** This is decisive for selection. A straight reprint earns 35% forever; a title where Valice-authored material is the *bulk* of the finished book can plausibly claim 70% and doubles the unit economics.

**[REC] Choose titles by how much of the finished book is yours, not by how good the old book is.**

### 3. Rights baseline **[FACT]**

- As of 1 Jan 2026, the US public domain covers works **published 1930 or earlier**. ([Duke CSPD, Public Domain Day 2026](https://web.law.duke.edu/cspd/publicdomainday/2026/))
- **A translation is a derivative work with its own copyright term** over the translator's expression (17 U.S.C. §101, [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/101)). The *Meditations* / George Long instinct was exactly right, and must be applied to every title below.
- Under *Bridgeman Art Library v. Corel*, 25 F. Supp. 2d 421 (S.D.N.Y. 1998), faithful photographic reproductions of 2-D public-domain artwork carry **no new copyright** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/25/421/2325910/)) — so library and museum scans of PD plates are usable. **[REC]** Still prefer CC0/PD-marked sources, since a restrictive terms-of-service claim is a contract question that *Bridgeman* does not dispose of.

## The ten titles

### 1. Werner, *Myths and Legends of China* (1922) — the best overall fit
**Edition [FACT]:** E. T. C. Werner (1864–1954), George G. Harrap, Feb 1922. **Written in English — no translator layer**, so none of the derivative-rights risk that sinks Gilgamesh and Popol Vuh.
**Rights [FACT]:** 1922 → US PD. Werner d. 1954 → life+70 expired 2024/25; clear in the EU/UK too.
**Source [FACT]:** Project Gutenberg **#15250**; Internet Archive `mythsandlegendso15250gut`. The 1922 original carried **32 full-page colour plates by Chinese artists** — *[needs verification]* whether the Gutenberg text release retains them; **[REC]** take plates from the IA page-image scan.
**Fit:** Literally the *Codex Mythologica* format, a century earlier, filling the largest market gap in the catalog.
**Original value [REC]:** Wade-Giles → **pinyin conversion throughout** (substantial, genuinely useful, copyrightable editorial work); a deity/creature cross-reference keyed to *Codex Bestiarium*; pronunciation guide; character-name concordance; and a modern note on Werner's dated, occasionally condescending 1922 framing — saying so is real scholarship, not disclaimer.
**Difficulty:** Low-medium. **Demand [FACT]:** continuously reprinted (Dover and others), and the competing editions are mostly undifferentiated reprints of this same text — meaning they are policy-vulnerable and yours would be the compliant one.

### 2. Falkener, *Games Ancient and Oriental and How to Play Them* (1892) — the closest brand match
**Edition [FACT]:** Edward Falkener (1814–1896), Longmans Green, 1892, 404 pp. English original, no translator.
**Rights [FACT]:** PD worldwide; life+70 expired 1966.
**Source [FACT]:** Internet Archive `gamesancientorie00falkuoft`, also `in.ernet.dli.2015.281418`. **Not on Project Gutenberg — no PG number exists**; treat any claimed one as false.
**Fit:** *The Great Book of World Games*' Victorian ancestor — same premise, same structure, same reader.
**Original value [REC]:** Redraw every board diagram in the house style already built for *World Games* — that alone satisfies KDP's 10-original-illustrations test. Add modernized playable rules (Falkener's Victorian rules prose is genuinely hard to follow), a component/setup appendix, printable board templates, and a "what archaeology corrected since 1892" layer — his senet reconstruction is now known to be wrong, which is an interesting annotation rather than filler.
**Difficulty:** Medium — but the redrawing *is* the differentiator, so none of the work is waste. **Demand [FACT]:** Dover kept it in print for decades (ISBN 0486207390); durable backlist, thin competition.

### 3. Topsell, *The Historie of Foure-Footed Beastes* (1607) & *Historie of Serpents* (1608)
**Edition [FACT]:** Use the combined 1658 *History of Four-Footed Beasts and Serpents*. Topsell's own English. Woodcuts largely derived from Gessner's *Historia Animalium* (1551–58), also PD.
**Rights [FACT]:** Unambiguously PD everywhere.
**Source [FACT]:** IA `historyoffourfoo00tops`, `topsellshistorie0000tops`; woodcuts at Univ. of Houston `n296x020p`. Gessner: IA `ConradiGesnerimIGess`, `ConradiGesnerimIVGess`, and the plates-only *Icones* at `iconesanimaliumq00gess`, which the **Smithsonian hosts under CC0 1.0** — the cleanest rights position available anywhere in this list.
**Fit:** *Codex Bestiarium*'s direct historical antecedent — a bestiary as reference, mixing real and legendary animals with total seriousness.
**Original value [REC]:** A **modernized-spelling edition**. The 1607 text is early-modern English and close to unreadable for a general buyer; modernizing it is itself substantial original editorial work, and it is the thing no competitor has done. Add a true / false / partly-true verdict box per creature, *Bestiarium* cross-references, and an essay on how the bestiary tradition transmitted errors for 1,500 years.
**Difficulty:** Medium-high — OCR of blackletter-adjacent type is poor and modernization is line-by-line. **[INFER]** Which is exactly why the field is empty, and exactly what earns the 70% tier.

### 4. Loyd, *Cyclopedia of 5000 Puzzles, Tricks and Conundrums* (1914)
**Edition [FACT]:** Sam Loyd (1841–1911), compiled posthumously by his son, Lamb Publishing, 1914 — the only edition.
**Rights [FACT]:** 1914 → US PD; life+70 long expired.
**Source [FACT]:** **Not on Gutenberg** — a direct catalog search returns no Loyd records. IA `samloydscycloped00saml`, `CyclopediaOfPuzzlesLoyd`, `samloydcyclopedi00libg`; partial Wikisource transcription.
**Fit:** *Codex Enigmatica*'s ancestor, and the bridge between the puzzle line and the reference line.
**Original value [REC]:** **Do not reprint 5,000 puzzles.** Curate ~150 that still work for a modern solver, redraw every diagram, write fresh solutions (Loyd's are terse and several are wrong), add difficulty ratings and a Loyd biography. Curation plus new solutions is genuinely majority-original.
**Difficulty:** Medium-high, mitigated entirely by curating down. **Demand [FACT]:** thin competition — a few POD facsimiles (~$10) plus Dover/Martin Gardner curated selections, which are long-running backlist and prove curated-Loyd sells. Original 1914 copies fetch ~$1,000.

### 5. Budge, *The Book of the Dead: The Papyrus of Ani* (1895)
**Edition [FACT]:** Use the **1895 facsimile edition** specifically, for the plates.
**Rights [FACT]:** 1895 → US PD; Budge d. 1934, life+70 expired 2005.
**Source [FACT]:** IA **`TheBookOfTheDead-Budge-1895`** — use this one; the identifier asserts the 1895 original. **Caution [FACT]:** IA `bookofdeadpapyru00budg` is catalogued as a **1967 Dover reprint** — avoid that scan. PG **#7145** is a Budge text edition signed 1920; treat as text-only.
**Illustrations [FACT]:** **37 folding colour facsimile plates.** **[INFER]** Doubly clean: a 3,000-year-old artifact, an 1895 facsimile, and no new copyright in the reproduction under *Bridgeman*.
**Original value [REC]:** Budge's translation is **known to be scholarly obsolete — make that the angle, not the liability.** Publish plate-led: the papyrus reproduced large, with a modern commentary track per vignette, a "where Budge got it wrong" apparatus, a glossary of the 42 negative confessions, and a spell-by-spell finding guide. The book becomes a guided reading of the papyrus, not a Budge reprint.
**Difficulty:** Medium-high (colour plates, large format). **Demand [FACT]:** best of any candidate — but **the heaviest competition too**, which is why it ranks 5th rather than 1st.

### 6. Culin, *Games of the North American Indians* (1907)
**Edition [FACT]:** Stewart Culin (1858–1929), 24th Annual Report of the Bureau of American Ethnology, Smithsonian, 1907. **A US federal government publication — PD by origin as well as by date. The strongest rights position in the study.**
**Source [FACT]:** IA `gamesofnorthamer00culirich`, `in.ernet.dli.2015.31459`. No Gutenberg edition found.
**Illustrations [FACT]:** **21 colour plates and 1,112 text illustrations** — the richest trove here.
**Companion [FACT]:** Culin's *Korean Games* (1895), IA `koreangameswith01culigoog`, `cu31924023272424` — 550 numbered copies with 22 colour lithographs, **some missing from some scans, so verify plate completeness**. **[INFER]** Pairs naturally with the Hangul workbook as a Korea mini-line.
**Original value [REC]:** Curate to ~60 playable games, write modern playable rules (Culin is ethnographic description, not rules), redraw implements and boards, and add the cultural-context and attribution apparatus a 1907 report does not have.
**⚠️ [REC] Engage Indigenous cultural advisors before publishing.** A 1907 salvage-ethnography text republished without that framing is a reputational risk, not merely an editorial gap. Treat this as a prerequisite with a real schedule cost, not a polish step — which is why this title is sequenced last.
**Difficulty:** High (~800 pp, image-only source, plus consultation).

### 7. Nivedita & Coomaraswamy, *Myths of the Hindus and Buddhists* (1913)
**Edition [FACT]:** Sister Nivedita (d. 1911) and Ananda Coomaraswamy (d. 1947), Harrap, 415 pp — a compiled English retelling, **no translator layer**.
**Rights [FACT]:** 1913 → US PD; both authors' life+70 expired (1981 / 2017).
**Source [FACT]:** **Not on Gutenberg.** IA `pli.kerala.rare.9411` (cleanest), `in.ernet.dli.2015.180814`, `dli.ministry.04598`.
**Illustrations [FACT]:** Watercolours supervised by **Abanindranath Tagore**, who drew some himself; pre-1930 → US PD, and his life+70 expired 2021. *[needs verification]* on the full illustrator credit list.
**Original value [REC]:** Sanskrit glossary with pronunciation; original deity relationship/avatar charts (KDP-compliant illustrations); a Ramayana/Mahabharata timeline apparatus; a modern note on the Vivekananda-circle framing.
**Difficulty:** Medium (OCR from IA). **Demand [FACT]:** Dover keeps it in print (9780486217598); HarperCollins India has an edition.

### 8. Dudeney, *The Canterbury Puzzles* (1907) & *Amusements in Mathematics* (1917) — start here
**Edition [FACT]:** Henry Ernest Dudeney (1857–1930). **Publication date controls US status** and both are pre-1930, so his 1930 death is irrelevant; life+70 also expired 2001.
**Source [FACT]:** Project Gutenberg **#27635** and **#16713** — both verified, with HTML/text/EPUB and the original diagrams included.
**Fit:** Direct *Codex Enigmatica* adjacency; *Canterbury Puzzles*' pilgrim frame-narrative suits the literary-puzzle reader.
**Original value [REC]:** Combine both into one curated volume, redraw diagrams, add difficulty tiers, add worked step-by-step solutions (Dudeney's are famously compressed), and add commentary on which puzzles became classics.
**Difficulty: Low — the easiest production in the top ten.**

### 9. Hearn, *Kwaidan: Stories and Studies of Strange Things* (1904) — start here too
**Edition [FACT]:** Lafcadio Hearn (d. 1904), Houghton Mifflin, 2 April 1904; English original.
**Rights [FACT]:** Clear everywhere; life+70 expired 1975.
**Source [FACT]:** Project Gutenberg **#1210**; IA `kwaidanstoriesst00hearuoft`. First edition illustrated by **Keishū Takénouchi** — US PD by publication date; *[needs verification]* on his death date for life+70 jurisdictions and on whether the IA scan retains the plates.
**Original value [REC]:** A yōkai field-guide apparatus cross-referenced to *Codex Bestiarium* — yuki-onna, mujina and rokurokubi all live in both books — plus original creature illustrations, a Japanese-terms glossary, and notes on Hearn's sources and where he embellished.
**Difficulty: Low** — short book, clean text.

### 10. *The Kalevala*, translated by W. F. Kirby (1907)
**Edition [FACT]:** Compiled by Lönnrot (d. 1884), 1835/1849. **Use Kirby's 1907 translation** (J. M. Dent). Kirby d. 20 Nov 1912 → life+70 expired 1982; pre-1930 → US PD. Clear in every jurisdiction.
**PD alternative [FACT]:** Crawford 1888, PG **#5186**, also clear — **[INFER] but Crawford translated from Schiefner's German, not from Finnish**: a translation of a translation, looser and less accurate. **[REC] Use Kirby.**
**Source [FACT]:** Project Gutenberg **#25953** (Vol. I) and **#33089** (Vol. II).
**Illustrations:** none attach to either translation. **[INFER/REC]** The fix is **Akseli Gallen-Kallela** (d. 1931), the definitive Kalevala painter — *Aino Triptych* (1891), *The Defense of the Sampo* (1896), *Lemminkäinen's Mother* (1897), the *Kullervo* series — all published/exhibited pre-1930 and therefore US PD, life+70 expired 2001. *[needs verification]* on a PD-marked high-resolution source per image.
**Original value [REC]:** Per-rune prose summaries, a character/place gazetteer, pronunciation guide, a Tolkien-influence essay (a live marketing hook), and the Gallen-Kallela plate programme with commentary.
**Difficulty:** Medium. **Demand [FACT]:** moderate, and notably **less crowded than the Edda or Bulfinch** — competition is just Kirby and Crawford reprints.

## Titles to avoid, and why **[FACT]**

- **Gilgamesh.** Every readable modern translation is in copyright (George 1999, Mitchell 2004, Sandars 1960). The PD options are Thompson 1928 (scholarly, archaic; IA `thompson-1928-gilgamesh`) or Hamilton 1884, a loose "restoration" that **invents material not in the tablets**.
- **Popol Vuh.** There is **no faithful complete PD English translation.** Lewis Spence's 1908 text (PG #56550) is a *paraphrase with commentary*, not a translation, and the market belongs to the copyrighted Tedlock and Christenson editions.
- **Grimm/Rackham and Aesop/Rackham.** PG #11339 (Aesop) is verified and clean, but both are saturated segments where the PD art is the only draw — and per finding 1 it earns no KDP credit.
- **Prose Edda, Brodeur 1916 translation.** US PD, **but Brodeur died in 1971, so it does not clear life+70 until 2042.** If you pursue Norse, use **Rasmus Anderson's 1879 translation, PG #18947**, which is clear worldwide.
