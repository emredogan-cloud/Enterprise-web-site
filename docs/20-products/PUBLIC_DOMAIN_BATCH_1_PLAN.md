# Public-Domain Batch 1 — the first three editions

> Batch 1 of the programme scored and sequenced in
> `PUBLIC_DOMAIN_8_10_BOOK_PLAN.md`. That document assessed twelve candidates
> and recommended ten; this one selects the first three, takes them to the end
> of pre-production, and stops before manufacture.
>
> Nothing here is produced yet. **Production status for all three is
> NOT STARTED**, and saying so is the point: a plan that claims a book exists
> is the failure mode this catalog spent two phases removing.

---

## What changed since that plan was written

Two things, and the second one changes the economics of the whole programme.

**1. Meditations is the live lesson.** The one public-domain title already
published is a Gutenberg text (George Long's 1862 *Meditations*, PG #15877),
newly typeset at 148 pages, priced **$9.99**. Its original contribution is
typesetting and a source note. Long's translation is free on Gutenberg and
$0.99 on Kindle. It is a real, honest edition — and it is at the top of its
price band on the strength of layout alone. Nobody has bought it, so there is
no data either way, but it is the concrete example of the trap this plan's
scoring was designed around: **the text being good is not the same as the
edition being worth buying.**

**2. Direct sale now works, and it inverts the royalty maths.**

The original plan reasoned inside KDP's constraint that public-domain content
is capped at the **35% royalty tier** unless the book carries substantial
original content. As of this session the Valice Press store sells ebooks
itself, end to end, verified in production. Paddle takes roughly 5% + $0.50.

| Channel | On a $9.99 ebook | Net |
|---|---|---|
| KDP, public-domain (35% tier) | 0.35 × 9.99 | **$3.50** |
| KDP, qualifies for 70% | 0.70 × 9.99 − delivery | ~$6.80 |
| **Direct, this store** | 9.99 − (5% + $0.50) | **~$8.99** |

**A public-domain edition sold here earns more than double what the same book
earns on KDP, and more than a book that fought its way to the 70% tier.**

That does not make the original-content work optional — it is what makes the
edition worth buying at all, and Amazon remains where the readers are. But it
changes the sequencing question from *"can this clear KDP's bar?"* to
*"is this good enough that someone would buy it from us directly?"* — a higher
bar, and a more useful one.

**Consequence for Batch 1: these three are direct-first.** Publish here, price
against the value of the apparatus rather than against Kindle's PD floor, and
treat KDP as the second channel once the edition has proved itself.

## Selection

| | Title | Why it is in Batch 1 | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1-1** | Dudeney, *The Canterbury Puzzles* (1907) + *Amusements in Mathematics* (1917) | Lowest-risk production in the whole programme, and the tightest adjacency to a book we already sell (*Codex Enigmatica*). Proves the pipeline cheaply. | **Low** |
| **B1-2** | Hearn, *Kwaidan* (1904) | Short, clean, and its creatures already appear in *Codex Bestiarium* — the cross-reference apparatus is the differentiator and it is half-built. | **Low** |
| **B1-3** | Falkener, *Games Ancient and Oriental and How to Play Them* (1892) | The closest brand match in the entire list: the Victorian ancestor of *The Great Book of World Games*. The differentiating work — redrawing every board in a house style we already have — is not overhead, it *is* the product. | **Medium** |

Werner's *Myths and Legends of China* scored highest overall (80.8) and is
deliberately **not** in Batch 1. It is the flagship, and a flagship should not
be the title you use to discover that your public-domain workflow has a
problem. It leads Batch 2.

## B1-1 · Dudeney — *The Puzzles of Henry Dudeney*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Rights** | **Clear worldwide.** Both volumes pre-1930 → US public domain by publication date. Dudeney d. 1930 → life+70 expired 2001. His death date does not control either way. |
| **Source edition** | *The Canterbury Puzzles and Other Curious Problems* (1907); *Amusements in Mathematics* (1917) |
| **Translator** | None — English original |
| **Archive IDs** | Project Gutenberg **#27635** and **#16713**. Both verified: HTML, plain text and EPUB, original diagrams included. |
| **Category** | Puzzle & Challenge |
| **Primary adjacency** | *Codex Enigmatica* |

**Editorial plan.** One curated volume selected from two, not a bind-up.
Dudeney published several hundred problems of wildly varying quality; the
edition is a *selection*, and the selection is the first piece of original
work. Target ~120 puzzles arranged by mechanism rather than by original book
order, keeping *Canterbury*'s pilgrim frame-narrative as the spine because it
is the thing that makes Dudeney literary rather than merely mathematical.

**Original contribution** — the part that is ours:
1. **Worked solutions.** Dudeney's own are famously compressed, often a single
   paragraph for a problem that needs a page. Rewriting them step by step is
   substantial original text and is the single most valuable thing this
   edition can offer.
2. **Difficulty tiers**, calibrated across the selection.
3. **Redrawn diagrams** in the house style — clears KDP's ten-original-
   illustrations test on its own.
4. **Commentary** on which puzzles entered the canon and where they went.
5. **A three-tier hint system**, matching the structure already built for
   *Codex Enigmatica*, so a reader moving between the two is on familiar ground.

**Cover strategy.** Original artwork in the Codex line's engraved idiom. No
public-domain illustration is used as the cover: KDP explicitly does not
accept public-domain images as differentiation, and it would put this edition
in a visual crowd it is trying to leave.

**Sample plan.** Four pages via the existing preview pipeline: one frame
narrative page, two puzzles at different tiers, one worked solution — because
the worked solution *is* the pitch.

**Pricing.** Direct **$9.99**; Kindle $9.99 if and when it goes to KDP; a
paperback is plausible later but is not Batch 1.

**Effort.** ~90–110 h. Selection 15 h · solution rewriting 45 h · diagrams
25 h · apparatus and typesetting 20 h.
**Finished composition (estimated): ~55% original** — solutions, commentary,
hints and diagrams against Dudeney's problem statements.

**Production status: NOT STARTED.**

## B1-2 · Hearn — *Kwaidan*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Rights** | **Text clear worldwide.** Hearn d. 1904; Houghton Mifflin, 2 April 1904; English original; life+70 expired 1975. |
| **Source edition** | *Kwaidan: Stories and Studies of Strange Things*, 1904 |
| **Translator** | None — Hearn wrote in English, retelling Japanese sources |
| **Archive IDs** | Project Gutenberg **#1210**; Internet Archive `kwaidanstoriesst00hearuoft` |
| **Category** | Myth & Folklore |
| **Primary adjacency** | *Codex Bestiarium* |

> **Open rights item.** The first edition's illustrations are by **Keishū
> Takénouchi**. US public domain by publication date is not in doubt; his
> death date is unconfirmed, so life+70 status in other jurisdictions is
> **unverified**, and whether the Internet Archive scan retains the plates is
> also unverified. **This must be settled before the plates are used, and the
> edition is designed not to need them** — see below.

**Editorial plan.** The seventeen *Kwaidan* stories complete and unabridged,
with Hearn's own "Studies of Strange Things" essays retained, plus an
apparatus that turns a story collection into a reference companion.

**Original contribution:**
1. **A yōkai field guide** cross-referenced to *Codex Bestiarium*. Yuki-onna,
   mujina and rokurokubi appear in both books; this edition is where the
   Valice bestiary and the Valice ghost stories meet, and no competing edition
   can do that because no competitor has the other book.
2. **Original creature illustrations** in the *Bestiarium* engraving style —
   which also removes the dependency on Takénouchi's plates entirely, turning
   an unresolved rights question into a non-issue rather than a risk.
3. **A Japanese-terms glossary** with pronunciation, on the model already
   proven in *The Great Book of World Myths*.
4. **Source notes** on what Hearn was retelling and where he embellished —
   genuinely interesting, and nowhere in print.

**Cover strategy.** Original artwork, *Bestiarium* line.

**Sample plan.** Four pages: the opening of *Hoichi the Earless*, one field-
guide entry with its plate, one glossary spread.

**Pricing.** Direct **$8.99**.

**Effort.** ~70–90 h. Field guide 30 h · illustrations 25 h · glossary and
notes 20 h.
**Finished composition (estimated): ~40% original.**

**Production status: NOT STARTED.**

## B1-3 · Falkener — *Games Ancient and Oriental*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Rights** | **Clear worldwide.** Falkener 1814–1896; Longmans Green, 1892; life+70 expired 1966. |
| **Source edition** | *Games Ancient and Oriental and How to Play Them*, 1892, 404 pp |
| **Translator** | None — English original |
| **Archive IDs** | Internet Archive `gamesancientorie00falkuoft`; also `in.ernet.dli.2015.281418`. **Not on Project Gutenberg — no PG number exists; treat any claimed one as false.** |
| **Category** | Games & Play |
| **Primary adjacency** | *The Great Book of World Games* |

**Editorial plan.** Falkener's scholarship survives; his prose does not. His
Victorian rules descriptions are genuinely hard to follow, and the whole
edition turns on fixing that.

**Original contribution:**
1. **Every board diagram redrawn** in the deterministic vector style already
   built and shipped for *The Great Book of World Games*. This is the largest
   single piece of work and it satisfies KDP's ten-illustration test many
   times over.
2. **Modernized playable rules** beside Falkener's originals — the reason
   somebody buys this edition instead of downloading the scan.
3. **A "what archaeology corrected since 1892" layer.** His senet
   reconstruction is now known to be wrong. That is an interesting annotation
   rather than filler, and it is the kind of thing that makes an old book
   worth reissuing rather than merely reprinting.
4. **A component and setup appendix**, plus **printable board templates** —
   which make the ebook edition genuinely more useful than the paper one, and
   this store sells ebooks.

**Cover strategy.** Original artwork in the *World Games* line, so the two
read as companion volumes on a shelf.

**Sample plan.** Four pages: one complete game entry showing original rules,
modernized rules and the redrawn board together — the three-column comparison
*is* the sales argument.

**Pricing.** Direct **$12.99**, matching *The Great Book of World Games*, which
it is explicitly a companion to.

**Effort.** ~140–170 h. Diagram redrawing 70 h · rules modernization 50 h ·
archaeology layer 20 h · appendix and templates 20 h.
**Finished composition (estimated): ~50% original** — and the strongest case in
Batch 1 for KDP's 70% tier if it goes there.

**Production status: NOT STARTED.**

## Batch economics

Assuming the direct channel and a blended $10.32 average across the three:

| | B1-1 Dudeney | B1-2 Kwaidan | B1-3 Falkener |
|---|---|---|---|
| Effort (h) | 100 | 80 | 155 |
| Direct price | $9.99 | $8.99 | $12.99 |
| Net per sale (direct) | ~$8.99 | ~$8.04 | ~$11.84 |
| Net per sale (KDP 35%) | $3.50 | $3.15 | $4.55 |
| Sales to 100 h-equivalent @ $25/h | 278 | 311 | 211 |
| Original share of finished book | ~55% | ~40% | ~50% |

**What this table is for.** It is not a forecast — there is no sales data for
any Valice Press title, and inventing one would be exactly the sort of number
this catalog has spent two phases deleting. It is a break-even shape, and the
shape says two things clearly:

- **Falkener needs the fewest sales to pay for itself** despite being the most
  work, because it carries the highest price and the tightest adjacency to an
  existing book. It is the best *business* case in Batch 1.
- **Dudeney is still the right one to build first**, because the purpose of
  B1-1 is to find out what is wrong with the workflow, and finding that out on
  a 100-hour book is much cheaper than finding it out on a 155-hour one.

## What must be true before any of the three enters production

1. `valicepress.com` registered — every edition carries the imprint and the
   verification-page pattern, and one book is already shipping with a dead
   address (see `CATALOG_MASTER_INVENTORY_FINAL.md`).
2. The **Meditations pricing question** answered. It is the same question
   these three face, on a book that already exists, and answering it there is
   free.
3. A written **house standard for original contribution** — the estimated
   percentages above should be measured against a rule, not asserted per book.
4. Takénouchi's death date verified, **or** the decision confirmed that B1-2
   uses only original illustrations. The plan currently assumes the latter.
5. The founder's **author/editor bio** written. `AUTHORS.bio` is deliberately
   null rather than invented, and a public-domain edition sells partly on who
   edited it.

## Not in Batch 1, and why

- **Werner, *Myths and Legends of China*** — highest score (80.8), and the
  flagship. Leads Batch 2, once the workflow has been proved on something
  cheaper.
- **Topsell, Loyd, Budge** — the three most capable of being majority-original
  work and of reaching the 70% tier, and the three most likely to consume a
  quarter before shipping. Batch 3.
- **Culin, *Games of the North American Indians*** — last, deliberately.
  Indigenous cultural consultation is a prerequisite for that title, not a
  finishing step, and it should not be started until there is time to do it
  properly.
