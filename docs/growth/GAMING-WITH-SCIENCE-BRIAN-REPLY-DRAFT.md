# Why this wording

**It answers his four points in his own order.** The episode, the PDF, the
Discord, the swag. A reply that reorders someone's email to suit the sender
reads as a form letter, and this is not one.

**It leads with the thing he offered to do, not the thing we want.** He said
he would share the boards link. So the boards link is the one that gets the
clear line of its own, and the book's page comes after it with an explicit
"if you ever want the book itself" — which gives him permission not to.
This inverts the brief's suggested link order deliberately: the landing page
is nominally primary, but the file he asked to share is the companion page,
and putting the shop first would have been answering a different email.

**Two links, both plain.** No UTM tags, no `?ref=`. He is going to paste one
of these into a Discord where people can see the URL; a tracking string on a
link a stranger is asked to trust is a small tax on trust for a measurement
we can already take from the referrer and the `companion_download` event.

**The miscount is corrected out loud.** The first email said 33 boards; the
pack has 31. This audience is three scientists and a Discord full of people
who care whether the Kurna board has a date. Quietly printing the right
number would have been fine; saying "I miscounted, and I fixed it" is
better, and it costs one parenthesis. The fix is real — three blog posts
carried the wrong number and no longer do.

**It repeats the reconstruction point, once.** It is the single thing this
book has that the competing family-games books do not, and it is the exact
thing that episode was about. Once is confident; twice would be selling.

**No ask.** No review, no rating, no promise-me-an-episode, no "would you
consider". The last line explicitly closes the loop: *if it is useful, that
is the whole of what I was after.* The offer to send Jason a copy is the
only forward motion in the email, and it is an offer, not a request.

**The swag line is a placeholder, not an address.** Brian offered; whether
to give a postal address is the Founder's decision and the Founder's data.
The draft cannot invent one and does not.

**Length.** Around 200 words. He wrote 60. A reply four times longer than the
message it answers is already pushing it.
