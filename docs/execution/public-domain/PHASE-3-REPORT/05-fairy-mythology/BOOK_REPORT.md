# Phase 3, Book 5 — *The Fairy Mythology*, in two volumes

**Thomas Keightley, 1828, enlarged 1850, reprinted 1892. Valice Classics 17 and 18.
Built 2026-09-07. Not deployed, not listed, not sold.**

---

## What it is

Keightley was a Dubliner who came to London in 1824 and made his living writing school
histories. In 1825 he helped Thomas Crofton Croker put together *Fairy Legends and Traditions
of the South of Ireland* — and the inquiries he made doing it turned into a book of his own.
*The Fairy Mythology* appeared in 1828, was translated into German almost at once, and drew a
letter of commendation from Jacob Grimm. Keightley reports that Goethe thought well of it. In
1850 he enlarged it greatly for Bohn; George Bell & Sons reprinted that setting from the
stereotype plates in 1892, and that is the text here.

It is the first book in English to put the fairy beliefs of Europe side by side and treat them
as one subject — Scandinavia, Germany, Switzerland, France, Spain, Italy, Ireland, Scotland,
England, Wales, and an appendix that reaches to Persia and the Talmud. Everything written in
English about comparative fairy belief for the next seventy years is downstream of it.

## Why two volumes

Keightley's text runs to **200,005 words**. Set the way this series sets a book, with the
apparatus a Valice Classic carries, that is something over six hundred printed pages: **past
KDP's 550-page hardcover ceiling outright**, and a paperback priced near $38.

The alternative to two volumes was never one volume. It was an abridgement — and Article 18
forbids cutting a book to make it convenient. So the book is divided **at Keightley's own
`GREAT BRITAIN` heading**, which is one of his top-level divisions and not an editorial
invention.

The halves fall at **99,871** and **100,134** of his words. That is a fact about how he built
the book, not about how it was cut: the seam was chosen for being his, and the balance is what
it happened to give.

## The apparatus is below the floor, and says so

**Volume I: 12.4%. Volume II: 9.7%. The floor is 20%.**

This is recorded, not padded. The Founder's standing instruction is that the 20% figure is a
quality floor and not a word-count target, and that where genuine apparatus falls short of it
the measured result is documented rather than faked. Reaching 20% here would have meant
writing **25,451 additional words** across the two volumes with nothing left to say.

What is actually there, per volume: a head-note before every single section; a Register of
Evidence and Inference; a glossary (59 terms in I, 31 in II); a register of the beings (25 in
I, 19 in II); a who's-who (20, 13); a chronology (21 rows, 20); a motif table; a tale index
(76 entries, 80); fifteen and fourteen editorial notes; twenty-four findings since; and six
and seven essays. Every section of a 200,000-word book is introduced. The share is low because
**the denominator is enormous**, not because the numerator is thin.

Recorded as **F-038**.

**These shares were corrected upward on 2026-09-07.** Writing this report turned up a defect
in `differentiation.py`, the instrument Article 2 is judged by: it counted every string in a
content file, so a verse block — which carries both a joined `text` for the matchers and its
`lines` for the typesetter — was counted **twice**. This book has 316 and 371 verse blocks;
10,175 and 10,853 of Keightley's words were being counted double, all of it in the SOURCE
denominator. The counter now skips structural keys and counts a block once, and all six
volumes of Phase 3 were re-measured. The shares here moved from 11.1% and 8.7% to 12.2% and
9.6% — still below the floor, and still recorded rather than padded.

## The rights position: five layers, and one refused

| | Layer | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| S-1 | Keightley's text, 1850 setting, 1892 reprint. **Keightley, 1789–1872** | public domain |
| S-2 | The **frontispiece** — a fairy montage. **George Cruikshank, 1792–1878** | public domain — **used** |
| S-3 | **Seven engraved script blocks**, one Persian couplet and six Sanskrit passages, set as engravings because the 1850 fount had no such sorts. Engraver not named | public domain — **used** |
| S-4 | PG ebook 41006, released 9 October 2012, produced by David Edwards, Delphine Lettau and the Online Distributed Proofreading Team | public domain — a faithful transcription attracts no new right |
| S-5 | **~150 one-letter glyph images** — insular Anglo-Saxon and Gaelic letterforms the 2012 transcribers set as PNGs | **NOT public domain — NOT USED** |

**S-2 took real work.** Bohn's 1850 single-volume issue replaced W. H. Brooke's 1828 etched
plates with a new frontispiece by Cruikshank. The plate in the source file carries a signature
at the lower right consistent with his — and the edition records that **the signature is
illegible at source resolution**, rather than claiming to have read it.

**S-5 is the layer that matters.** Those letter-images are 2012 work by identifiable living
hands, not Keightley's of 1850, and this edition has no right to them. Refusing them
*improved the book*: **141 glyphs became their Unicode characters**, so the Old English and
Irish in this edition can be searched, copied, resized and read aloud by a screen reader —
none of which a picture of a letter permits.

The mapping was established by reading each glyph **in the word it spells** — *Munt-ælfen*,
*Sǽ-ælfen*, *Wulfes-fist*, *daine maiṫ*, *siaḃra* — never from a file name and never from a
shape alone. The build **refuses to run** if it meets an image that is neither a mapped glyph
nor a listed plate, so a new glyph cannot enter silently.

## Verification

**20 claims registered. 20 verified. 0 unverifiable. 2 corrected at verification.**

**One claim in twenty cites Wikipedia at all, and none cites it alone.** That is the British
Goblins lesson applied: in that book eleven of twenty-five verified claims rested on Wikipedia
as sole evidence, and the adversarial review found eleven falsehoods. Here the primary source
is the instrument and the secondary source corroborates.

**20 source-claims asserted against the source by `check_source_claims.py`, 0 failed** — the
checker this phase built, running on the book it was built after.

The two corrections:

- **C-018 — a spliced quotation.** The essay on Keightley and Croker had joined two of his
  phrases into one set of quotation marks: *"aided in that work, both in text and notes"* — a
  sentence he never wrote. `check_quotes.py` caught it, but only after the run that mattered:
  the checker had reported **"checked: 0"** against this book, because Keightley's apparatus
  quotes in italics rather than in marks, and a checker that finds nothing to check passes.
  An opt-in `--italics` mode was added, and it immediately found the splice. The passage now
  quotes the two phrases separately.
- **C-020 — the engravings, corrected twice.** The first parse found **one of eight**: six
  live inside footnotes, where the inline walker returns nothing for an image, one sits in a
  div beside its footnote rather than inside it, and the frontispiece stands before the title
  page. All eight are now placed.

## What else the build had to be taught

- **283 `[Pg iv]` page markers were printing inside sentences.** Inline `span.pagenum`
  elements, invisible to a walker looking at block level. Stripped at parse.
- **604 footnotes, 79 of them orphaned.** Anchors were being collected from `<p>` only —
  but 76 of these are called from verse stanzas, 6 from headings and 1 from an `h6`. The
  collector now reads anchors from every element. **604 placed, 0 orphans.**
- **A footnote guard that discarded a figure.** The seen-set was marking a footnote id on its
  first block rather than emitting all of it, so an engraving that followed the first
  paragraph of a note vanished.
- **346 poems were being set as prose,** lines joined with " / " — the whole of *Proud
  Margaret* arriving as one grey paragraph. Verse now renders its lines.

## The editorial centre of Volume II

Buried in the Ireland section, mid-paragraph, without emphasis, Keightley writes that some of
the traits the Grimms called characteristic of Irish fairies **"owe their origin to the fancy
of the writers"** — writers who were, he says, more anxious to produce amusing tales than to
transmit legends faithfully. He is one of those writers. He is telling the reader that a part
of the Irish fairy canon was invented, by people including himself, and that the Grimms
repeated it as folklore.

Volume II's apparatus is built around that admission. It is the reason the Register in this
volume carries a fifth column no other book of this phase has needed: **what the author had a
hand in making.**

## A heuristic that was built, run, and thrown away

The tale index was to carry a column naming each tale's collector, assigned by
nearest-preceding authority in the text. It worked, mostly — and then attributed a Persian
tale to Saxo Grammaticus, because the nearest authority above it happened to be Saxo. The
column was **dropped rather than shipped with a footnote**, and the index introduction says
why: a heuristic that is right nine times in ten produces a reference work that is wrong in a
way the reader cannot detect.

## What the adversarial review found

The reviewer was given one instruction — *prove these two volumes are not ready* — and returned
**NOT READY with six P0 findings**. Every one was confirmed independently before it was acted
on, and two of them were things no check in this factory could have caught.

### P0-1 · 3,363 words of Keightley's footnotes were being thrown away

**The worst finding of the phase.** A footnote is not one paragraph, but only its *first*
paragraph carries the `Footnote_N` anchor — and the extractor did `if not anchor: continue`.
Every continuation was discarded: **61 notes truncated, 19 printed as nothing at all.**

Volume I page 38 ended footnote 31 at *"The poet Sâdee, to express the bounty of the Almighty
says"* — full stop, nothing after it. Footnote 14, the Provençal *fadaro/fadatz* evidence that
is the etymological core of *Origin of the Word Fairy*, printed blank. So did Massinger's
*Fatal Dowry* at 400 and Heywood's *Hierarchie* at 549.

**Four checks were watching and none of them could see it.** The parse report counted notes
*found* — containers, not contents — and stayed at 604 the whole time. `coverage.py` compares
each section's first and last **prose** paragraph. `check_quotes.py` reads the apparatus, not
the source. And Volume I page 4 told the reader, in print: *nothing is abridged.*

Fixed at the parser, along with two shapes it had never met: a note whose entire body is a
poem (nineteen of them), and footnote 490, whose body is a **two-column table** of Welsh
against Breton — `table` was not in the footnote walk at all, and that note printed as a bare
number. The build now **refuses to complete** if any note would reach the page empty, which is
what page 5 had been claiming all along that it did.

A new source-claim, **SC-021**, asserts the far end: the *words* inside the notes, as a floor.
Its regression test is the broken extractor's own figure, 18,196.

### P0-2 · William Hone was a fabricated authority

Both volumes gave him a citation count — fourteen and twenty-six — and Volume II carried a
**whole essay** built on him, *England and the Every-Day Book*, with the sentence *"Hone is
cited twenty-six times here — more than Grimm, more than Scott."*

**He is named zero times in either volume.** `Every-Day Book` and `Table Book` appear nowhere
in the source. The only near-hits are *Honest Whore*, *Honest Man's Fortune* and the *Poor but
Honest So'dger*.

Every other count in the same table was exact. This one was invented, marked VERIFIED, and
threaded through the glossary, the register, the who's-who, the editorial notes, the
head-note to England, the companion copy and the claims ledger.

What is actually there was worth finding: **the English section rests on poets, not
collectors** — Milton ten, Chaucer nine, Spenser seven, Drayton six, Jonson five — and on
**Brand's *Popular Antiquities***, cited nine times, itself a compilation of earlier print.
Keightley is most at home in England and least able to say who told him. That is now the
editorial note, and **SC-023** holds the book to it.

### P0-3 · Walter Scott's counts, and a book he never cites

Claimed fifteen and sixteen citations. Measured: seven and six occurrences of the *name*, of
which one in Volume I is **Jonathan Scott** the translator, one in Volume II is **Reginald
Scot** of the *Discoverie of Witchcraft*, and several are stanza attributions. And the *Letters
on Demonology and Witchcraft* — named in both entries — **is never cited**. The only
*Demonologie* in the book is King James's.

### P0-4 · The Kalevala

Volume II told the reader its Finnish section *depends on the Kalevala*. `Kalevala`,
`Kalewala` and `Lönnrot`: **zero occurrences.** The section rests on *Mnemosyne* (Åbo, 1821)
at second hand through Grimm, and on Rühs. The entry now says exactly that — it is an entry
about an **absence**, which is more interesting than the claim it replaced — and **SC-024**
keeps it honest.

### P0-5 · The wrong transcriber was named

`RIGHTS.md` credited *David Edwards*, in the row that refuses the transcribers' work, with the
note that the names are as the bibrec gives them. The bibrec, the HTML and the plain text all
read **Douglas L. Alley, III**. Article 4 requires a record that names a person; the person was
wrong.

### P0-6 · Hebrew was dropped from both printed volumes

The running face has no Hebrew coverage and **ReportLab drops uncovered glyphs without an
error**. Three of Keightley's etymological notes printed with their subjects missing —
*"from ... to lay waste, Deut. xxxii. 17."* — and the Chaldee epigraph of Psalm xci printed as
its citation and nothing else. Twelve runs in the content files; zero in either PDF.

Fixed by registering Noto Serif Hebrew — and then a **second** failure surfaced underneath it:
**ReportLab has no bidi.** It sets glyphs left to right in string order, so Hebrew handed to it
in logical order comes out *mirrored*: right letters, right font, read backwards. Caught by
holding the extracted text against the source and finding the runs present only in reverse.
All eleven now read correctly.

### The P1s, and what they cost

Nineteen further findings were fixed. The ones worth naming:

- **49 verse blocks printed a doubled attribution** — *"Stagnelius. Stagnelius."*,
  *"Chaucer. Chaucer."* — because `find_all("span")` is recursive and a nested attribution was
  emitted twice. Nearly every section epigraph in the pair.
- **Footnote markers leaked into section titles**, and from there into the running heads, the
  TOC, the motif concordance **and the head-note keys** — `Oriental Romance. [22]`.
- **The 604 notes printed with no numbers at all.** The label was extracted at parse and
  discarded, so the reader met `Shedeem,[587]` in the text and unlabelled lines below it.
- **The glyph essay stated something false about the source.** It said *þ, ð and æ* were
  already characters and untouched. There is **no ð** anywhere, and all **four þ** are the
  transcribers' misreading of insular **wynn** — each provable from Keightley's own gloss in
  the same sentence (*dƿeorg* "thence dwerke", *ƿǽn* "not a contraction of waggon", *cƿen*
  "whence queen and quean", *ƿeordig* "worthy"). Read as thorn, every one is a word that does
  not exist. All four are corrected, declared under Article 18, and guarded by **SC-022**.
- **Six of the seven engravings were called Sanskrit** — lifted from the transcriber's alt
  text, the one source `RIGHTS.md` says this edition does not rely on. Keightley's own
  sentences give **Persian** for the Sâdee couplets and the sīmurgh and **Arabic** for the
  last. Where he does not name the language, the map now says so instead of guessing.
- **Volume II's companion page inventoried Volume I's illustrations**, byte-identically.
- **Volume II's glossary carried Volume I's title** — *the Northern Words*, over
  pouke, pixy, brownie, banshee, leprechaun, korrigan, duende.
- **The hardcover spine broke the wrap margin on both volumes** — 0.022 in of clearance where
  0.125 in is required, because `spine()` measured from the sheet and on a case the outer
  0.591 in of the sheet is the fold. The panel builders had been given a safe inset when this
  was found for them; the spine had not. Now 0.136 in and 0.139 in.
- **The two spines were the same object twice.** Same ground, same title, same author,
  differing only by 0.023 in of width. A line reading
  `CFG["project"]["spineTitle"] = CFG["project"]["spineTitle"]` sat exactly where the
  per-volume lift belonged and did nothing. Both spines and both fronts now carry a volume
  mark set at title scale, so the pair can be told apart on a shelf and in a thumbnail.
- **`"iccProfile": "sRGB embedded"` was written unconditionally** for four print wraps that
  had none. ReportLab strips the JPEG's APP2 marker when it writes the page, so passing
  `icc_profile=` to the image save was necessary and not sufficient. The profile is now
  attached to the PDF as an OutputIntent, and **the record reads the value back out of the
  files** instead of asserting it.
- **The cover QA note described another book's painting** — *"a moonlit Welsh valley whose
  whole subject is the one small flame on the path"*. That is *British Goblins*. Volume I is a
  Norse mound over a night fjord; Volume II a standing stone above an estuary at dusk. It was
  the only justification offered for both volumes missing the house title floor, so a
  measurement was being excused with a different book's facts.
- **The QR record had gone stale** because it was written by a separate script that no longer
  exists — it placed the code on page 331 of a book that is 336 pages long. The measurement
  now happens inside the builder, locates the page by its heading, and reads the code's size
  and its URL back with a detector that did not write it.

### What the review confirmed

The volume split closes: `GREAT BRITAIN` is a top-level heading, nothing straddles the seam,
nothing is duplicated, no tale title is lost. The arithmetic closes to **within 49 words on
200,000**. The footnote anchoring is a perfect bijection, 604 to 604. **All nineteen glyph
mappings are correct**, checked against the words they spell — including the three dotted
Irish letters, where the reviewer independently derived *daine maiṫ* → *deenè mâh* and
*siaḃra* → *shifra*. The refused layer is genuinely absent from both PDFs and both EPUBs: zero
md5 matches against the source PNGs. And the Confession — the volume's headline claim, its
cover pull-quote and its blurb — is verbatim where the edition says it is.

## What is not done

Neither volume is on sale, and neither can be. Both are blocked on the Founder's **Gate 2
(Rights)** signature, which no agent can give — the S-2 attribution and the S-5 refusal are
exactly the kind of judgment that signature exists for. No Paddle product, no R2 master, no
KDP listing. **No ASIN, ISBN or identifier has been invented for either volume.**

The printed companion addresses — `valicepress.com/companion/fairy-mythology-vol-1` and
`-vol-2` — are permanent once a book is printed, and the branch that serves them is
undeployed. They 404 against production until Phase 3 ships.
