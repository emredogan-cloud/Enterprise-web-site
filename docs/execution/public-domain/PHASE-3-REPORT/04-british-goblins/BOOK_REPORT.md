# Phase 3, Book 4 — *British Goblins*

**Wirt Sikes, 1880, with illustrations by T. H. Thomas. Valice Classics 16. Built 2026-09-06.
Not deployed, not listed, not sold.**

---

## What it is

In June 1876 the United States sent a consul to Cardiff. Wirt Sikes was a New York journalist
of thirty-nine who had written two novels and, by one account, used as many as thirty
pseudonyms. He spent four years in Wales asking what people were afraid of after dark, and in
1880 he published the largest book on Welsh folklore in English. Almost everything a general
reader knows about the *tylwyth teg* comes through it, usually without their knowing it.

Richard Dorson called it the most substantial book of Welsh legendry in English and, in the
same assessment, said what is wrong with it: Sikes leans on earlier compilations — above all
on Edmund Jones's Monmouthshire collection of 1780 — and stops marking them at the point of
use. Four hundred pages arrive in one voice, and a reader cannot tell eighteenth-century
Monmouthshire from the countryside he was living in. John Rhŷs and E. S. Hartland objected on
exactly that ground and cited him anyway. This edition exists to do the marking for him.

## What this edition adds

- **A head-note before each of the thirty-two chapters**, each ending in **Reading against
  him** — the specific thing in that chapter to resist.
- **An introduction to each of the four Books**, because the four were made by four different
  methods and that is the fact a reader most needs.
- **A Register of Evidence and Inference**, arranged by Book rather than by chapter for the
  same reason: what he watched, what he was told, what he read, what he concluded.
- **A Welsh glossary of fifty entries** which, with the register of the beings, explains some
  sixty Welsh terms — with how to say them. The introduction argues this is the single most
  useful thing the edition adds, and that the need for it is a comment on the original.
- **What Has Been Established Since 1880** — eleven findings. Three sustained, four not
  sustained, one abandoned, one still disputed, one not a finding at all, and one true only
  with a large qualification.
- A descriptive list of every plate, the six airs named and placed, a gazetteer of the
  parishes, a who's-who, a pronunciation guide, a chronology, editorial notes, and six essays.

## The rights position, and the layer that is refused

This is the first book of the phase with an illustration layer that is **used**, and the first
with one that is **refused**.

- **S-1 · the text.** Sikes 1836–1883, published London 1880. Public domain on two independent
  grounds; the later of the two governing dates is 1985, from the illustrator.
- **S-2 · the drawings.** T. H. Thomas 1839–1915, published in the same volume, named in his
  own field on the Gutenberg bibliographic record and on the 1880 title page. Twenty of the
  twenty-one are set.
- **S-3 · the transcription.** PG 34704, released 20 December 2010, last updated 7 January 2021.
  A faithful transcription attracts no new right; header, licence and trademark stripped.
- **S-4 · the music engravings. NOT public domain, NOT used.** The six tunes in the Gutenberg
  file are not scans of Sikes's 1880 plates. They were set in 2010 by Lesley Halamek, whom the
  bibliographic record credits by name for exactly that work. They are not reproduced. Each air
  is instead named, placed where Sikes put it, and described in the apparatus, and the build
  asserts the six files by name rather than trusting that they were left out.

The temptation this layer represents is the one Article 3 exists to defeat: an image sitting
inside a public-domain file is not thereby public domain. The old ninety-four-row research pool
made that error eight times, four of them on the illustration layer.

## What verification changed

Seven of the twenty-seven registered claims were **corrected rather than confirmed**. The ones
that mattered:

**The Pontypridd ceremonies were not fifty years old.** Six passages — the who's-who, an
editorial note, the gazetteer, a head-note, the sources table and *What Has Been Established
Since 1880* — said Sikes watched a revival "about fifty years old", and one said he "misdated
it by fifty years or so". The Dictionary of Welsh Biography gives Myfyr Morganwg beginning
services at the Maen Chwyf **c. 1853**; Sikes arrived in June 1876. They were about
twenty-three years old — younger than he was, and inside the memory of anyone in the parish
over thirty. The error made his misreading look more forgivable than it is.

**Lady Charlotte Guest did not give English the word *Mabinogion*.** The who's-who and the
chronology both said she did. The book's own glossary, three sections away, already said the
opposite and was right: the plural is a scribal error that was current among Welsh scholars
before her (Pughe, 1795). A volume that contradicted itself on its own page.

**The 1870s were not a decade of open official pressure on the Welsh language.** The
introduction and a head-note both said so. The Welsh Not was commonest earlier in the century
and was already fading by Sikes's decade. What is true of his years, and is the fact that
actually bears on a collector's position, is that virtually all schooling in Wales was in
English and Welsh had no standing in law. Corrected to that.

**Sikes was thirty-nine when he was appointed, not forty** — born 23 November 1836, appointed
June 1876. His age at death, forty-six, was right.

**Dorson was writing in the 1960s, not "seventy years later"** — which would have put him in
1950. *The British Folklorists* is 1968–69.

**Holywell's claim was vaguer than the evidence and is now sharper.** Five passages called it
"the one Welsh pilgrimage site with a continuously documented history" — documented from when?
The sourced fact is narrower and stronger: it is unique among Britain's sacred sites in keeping
its pilgrimage unbroken through the Reformation.

**The findings section miscounted its own findings.** It summarised "Ten findings. Four
sustained… four not sustained." The list above it has eleven, numbered; three are sustained,
four are not sustained, one is abandoned, one still disputed, one not a finding, and one true
only with a qualification. The fourth "sustained" it named was not one of the numbered findings
at all.

## What else was found and fixed in the build

- **An HTML comment printed as body text.** `<!-- blank page -->` sits inside the page anchor
  for p.396 of the transcription. BeautifulSoup returns comment text from `get_text()` like any
  other string node, so the words **blank page** were set as a paragraph in the middle of Book
  IV chapter V, and the built PDF printed them. One comment in the file, and it shipped.
  Comments are now stripped before anything reads text out of the tree.
- **Translation glosses set as quotations.** Nine English renderings of Welsh terms — *the
  mothers' blessing*, *woman of Annwn*, *changed child*, the tune titles — were inside double
  quotation marks, which say "these are somebody's words". In a book whose whole argument is
  that Sikes does not mark his sources, that is the one mistake the apparatus may not make.
- **Words put in Sikes's mouth.** The Register twice quoted him as not writing "I watched
  this". He never wrote those three words; quoting them in the passage that faults him for
  unmarked attribution was the defect the passage is about. Rewritten to say what he does not
  do without inventing what he might have said.
- **The plate captions were counted as the editor's words.** `plates.json` carries alt text,
  filenames, placements *and* Sikes's own printed captions. Measuring the whole file as
  apparatus counted 140 of his words as ours. The printed captions are now split into their
  own file and counted as source, the way Sea Monsters counts Lee's.
- **Six music engravings carried the next plate's caption.** The positional walker hands an
  uncaptioned image the caption that follows it — the bug that was fixed for the ornaments and
  not for the music. Nothing wrong was printed, because the music is not set; a false caption
  left in a content file is a thing that gets printed later.
- **The QR was measured, not assumed.** A 2.55 in image draws a 2.14 in code once the
  four-module quiet zone is taken off — 23.8% of the page height, under the 25% floor, while
  the file looked correct. The image is now sized from the code outward: 2.31 in measured off
  the finder patterns by a reader that did not write it.
- **The back-cover copy overran into the barcode zone.** The first wrap put 1,612 bright pixels
  in the lower-right 2.0 × 1.2 in where KDP prints the barcode, and collided with the imprint
  line. The blurb was cut from 1,963 to 1,547 characters; the zone is now clear at zero pixels.
- **The glossary was credited with "some sixty Welsh words".** It has fifty entries, four of
  them English. Fifty-nine distinct Welsh terms are explained, across the glossary *and* the
  register of beings. Stated that way now.

## What is not done

- **Not deployed.** Phase 3 is unmerged by instruction. The companion address is printed
  permanently in the book and 404s against production until this branch ships. That is a
  release blocker and is recorded as one on the catalogue row.
- **No Paddle product, no R2 master, no KDP listing.** `paddlePriceId`, `masterFileKey` and
  `epubFileKey` are null and `kdp` is `not_created`. No identifier is invented for any.
- **Gate 2 unsigned** — four rows, and the first illustration layer of the phase that is used.
  **F-035.**
- **Two claims are marked UNVERIFIABLE rather than verified.** That the aboriginal-race theory
  has no evidential support, and that the sin-eater remains disputed. Both are filed in the book
  as editorial interpretation under Article 6 rather than asserted as current fact, and the
  ledger records that no single citable authority was read for either.
- **The apparatus clears the floor narrowly** — 20.14%, a margin of roughly 150 words. Recorded
  rather than widened: Article 2 forbids padding to reach the number, and the book already has
  twenty-seven back-matter sections. Any future cut must re-measure.
- **Pricing is high and untested.** $22.99 paperback and $41.99 hardcover are the price engine's
  own recommendations for 388 pages at the 35% target, and no book this press has sold is priced
  anywhere near them. **F-036.**
- **Cover series idiom unresolved** — a painted cover where COVER_STANDARDS gives Valice
  Classics as typographic. Title band 17.8% of cover height against a 25% rule; recorded as
  measured and not met. The same open Founder decision as the other three books (**F-031**).
