# The selection, and why it is this one

**Volume One prints the five chapters of *Puzzles Old and New* that a reader can actually
solve tonight, with a pen, a handful of coins and a box of matches. The five chapters that
require you to own a Victorian puzzle — a bent-wire trick, a dissected block, a trick box —
are not in it.**

The roadmap's instruction for this title was explicit: *"Puzzles Old and New is the heaviest
single production in the catalogue — several hundred engravings. Scope it as a selection,
never as a complete edition."* This is that selection, and the line was drawn by measurement
rather than by taste.

## What was measured

Every chapter's puzzle half and its key half were counted together: words, and distinct
`Fig.` references in the OCR.

| Ch | Title | Figures | Words | In Volume One? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| I | Puzzles Dependent on Dexterity and Perseverance | 25 | 4,080 | No |
| II | Mechanical Puzzles Dependent on some Trick or Secret | 78 | 11,728 | No |
| III | "Dissected" or Combination Puzzles | **194** | 7,802 | No |
| **IV** | **Arithmetical Puzzles** | 33 | 25,605 | **Yes** |
| **V** | **Word and Letter Puzzles** | 10 | 4,253 | **Yes** |
| **VI** | **Puzzles with Counters** | 29 | 4,456 | **Yes** |
| **VII** | **Puzzles with Lucifer Matches** | 19 | 1,999 | **Yes** |
| VIII | Wire Puzzles | 22 | 1,969 | No |
| **IX** | **"Quibble" or "Catch" Puzzles** | 0 | 3,125 | **Yes** |
| X | Miscellaneous Puzzles | 69 | 14,886 | No |

**Selected: 91 figures, 39,438 words. Excluded: 378 figures, 40,465 words.**

That is the whole argument in one line. The two halves are almost the same size in words —
49.4% against 50.6% — and utterly unlike in what they cost to produce and in what they give a
reader. The half that can be solved with household objects carries **19% of the figures**.

## Why the excluded chapters are excluded

Not because they are worse. Because they are unusable.

Chapter III's puzzles are dissected wooden blocks sold by Victorian toy dealers: the Anchor
Puzzle, the sectional cubes. Chapter II's are trick boxes and barrels with concealed catches.
Chapter VIII's are bent-wire tanglements. Chapter I's are dexterity toys — you tilt a glass
case and roll a ball into a hole. **You cannot do any of them by reading about them.** Hoffmann
could assume a reader who could walk into a shop and buy one; that shop closed a century ago.

Reprinting 378 engravings of toys nobody can obtain, recovered from a 130-year-old library
scan, would produce a thick book that no reader can use — and it is exactly what every
existing reprint of this title does.

Chapter X is excluded for a different and more honest reason: it is genuinely mixed. Some of
its forty puzzles are paper-and-pencil and some are apparatus. Splitting it would mean either
importing apparatus puzzles into a volume that promises none, or cutting a chapter in half
without saying so. It goes whole into Volume Two.

## What the reader gets

**161 puzzles**, complete and unaltered in Hoffmann's 1893 words, with his own solutions:

- **Chapter IV, Arithmetical** — 95 puzzles, the largest in the book, and the one modern
  puzzle books still quarry. The Market-woman and her Eggs is here.
- **Chapter V, Word and Letter** — 15, including the puzzling inscriptions.
- **Chapter VI, Counters** — 11, playable with any coins.
- **Chapter VII, Lucifer Matches** — 3 head puzzles that expand into a set of matchstick
  problems in the key.
- **Chapter IX, "Quibble" or "Catch"** — 37 verbal catches, which need nothing at all.

## What Valice adds

- Every figure **redrawn from code**. No scanned engraving is reproduced anywhere in this
  edition — see `RIGHTS.md`, Layer 4. A 1893 line engraving pulled off a grayscale library
  scan prints as grey mush; these are drawn clean, to a single specification, at print
  resolution.
- A **difficulty rating** on every puzzle, and the reasoning behind the scale.
- A **register of what is still true**: Hoffmann's arithmetic is sound and his word puzzles
  are period-bound. Where a puzzle depends on a Victorian assumption — a currency, a spelling,
  a unit — it is marked, because a reader who cannot solve it should know it is the century's
  fault and not theirs.
- A stated **note on the text** naming the edition, the archive item, the library that scanned
  it, and exactly which five chapters were removed and why — this document, in the book.
- **Cross-references to Codex Enigmatica**, the live Valice puzzle volume this shelf sits beside.

## The honest statement of scope

The book's front matter says, on its first page and not its last, that this is **Volume One of
two**, that it contains five of Hoffmann's ten chapters, and which five. Phase 3 settled that
convention on *Myths and Legends of China*: a selection announces itself at the front.

**Volume Two — the apparatus puzzles — is not promised with a date.** It requires 378 original
figures, and it will be scoped when there is a way to draw them that is worth a reader's money.
